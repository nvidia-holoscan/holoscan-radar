- **2026-08-04** — Autofocus grid conjugacy closed as **D20**, was R4, the last of the practitioner critique's ranked design wounds still untouched. `AutofocusOp.phase_correction` becomes **per-pulse** rather than per-azimuth-bin, and a new `PhaseCorrectionOp` applies it to range profiles ahead of formation — so PGA's conjugate-geometry requirement binds the image it estimates *from* and never the product. The recommended graph estimates on a small auxiliary conjugate-grid image and forms the product once on the DEM or ground lattice, which keeps direct map-geometry formation and autofocus in the same chain at a few percent rather than double the formation cost.
- The unlock for D20 was recognizing that the error is per-pulse and only the *estimator* is per-geometry, so publishing in azimuth bins had been encoding the estimator's coordinate system in the port. Wave one grows to nine operators; the residual hazard, that the conjugacy precondition is declared through `azimuth_axis` and cannot be checked, is recorded with the decision.
- **2026-08-04** — The sample geometry's lack of a batch axis recorded as **D19**, deferred rather than rejected. A batch is a batch of *collections*, not of *scenes*, so a tomographic volume ([SAR/WORKFLOWS.md](SAR/WORKFLOWS.md) mode 9) is `N` formation instances even though its height planes share pulses, weights and aperture boundaries and differ only in geometry. Letting `sample_points` carry a leading axis the data does not — making the output batch the outer product of the two — is the additive change that would collapse them into one instance. Wide swath (mode 6) needs separate instances regardless, its sub-swaths closing apertures at different pulses.
- **2026-08-05** — The three remaining SAR design questions closed together, because each was the other two's fallback. **D21** (was R5) **declines a descriptive metadata plane**: the writers' mirrored geometry, frame and window parameters stay the ordinary path, and the silent-disagreement hazard is accepted under invariant 5 rather than designed out. What is offered instead is an escape hatch built from machinery already in the contract — three optional descriptor *output* ports (`CPHDReaderOp.coordinate_frame`, `SARRangeCompressionOp.range_window`, `BackprojectionOp.sample_geometry`) wirable into the consumer's same-named `[live]` override, where the port supersedes the parameter and a disagreement warns once.
- **D22** (was R7) gives the boundary announcement a producer and a carrier: a finite source stamps `holoscan.radar.stream_boundary` as message metadata, and whoever drives `control` translates it. A port could not do this, because a non-blocking flag races the data and a blocking one holds alignment only through 1:1 stages, which `ApertureManagerOp` is not. **D23** (was R2) fixes the control message at the four values and nothing else, R2's stake having been whether a writer could label its product without the plane D21 declined.
- Two consequences are worth knowing outside the SAR docs. `kUpdate` is mechanically forced rather than preferred — one dictionary per producer means a consumer taking several ports from it merges the same content repeatedly, and `kRaise` fires on a key being present, not on values disagreeing — but it binds **one operator, not the application** (see the 2026-08-06 correction below; this entry originally claimed otherwise). `ApertureManagerOp` now reads the announcement itself, so five of its six configurations are a pure YAML edit where two previously needed an application-owned `control` edge just to avoid dropping a collection's final aperture.
- And **R10 is raised**: the key namespace (`holoscan.radar.<key>`) is proposed module-wide because a finite FMCW source loses its tail the same way, which makes it a decision about FMCW taken in a SAR document — flagged in [TEAM_BLOCKED.md](TEAM_BLOCKED.md) for FMCW owners.
- **2026-08-06** — SAR input data written up as [SAR/INPUT_DATA.md](SAR/INPUT_DATA.md), covering both the generated and the public-collection paths. Fixture policy: **no CPHD binaries in git** (CI and demo fixtures alike generate from committed recipes), the simulator **lives in this repository and is public** because users are expected to generate their own input, and `sarpy` is confined to the generate/pull path. All three supersede [SAR/TESTING.md](SAR/TESTING.md)'s external-simulator and committed-fixture language, and **that amendment has landed**: the version pin is dropped, bit-for-bit is scoped to one toolchain, and CI generates its fixture as a build step. Credential-free public collections are pinned for first-ship modes 1–5; the two Capella keys still need re-verifying.
- The mode 2 video SAR demo takes the **full 23.5 s Houston collect**, validated end to end offline against the MatX `sar_bp` example: 6.84 GB pulled in 7.1 min (request-rate bound — 128 keep-alive connections is the measured optimum, 256 regresses), 410 frames at 17.7 fps formed in 14 s of GPU, and 20 confirmed moving vehicles in the 3.6 s burst the mover analysis covered. `matx::experimental::sar_bp` stays a golden-image path and **not a build dependency**, per I1 — it is absent from the MatX bundled with our SDK.
- Two findings land on the operator work rather than the data. A near-broadside spotlight has `omega * R / V` = 1, so a ground mover's apparent image velocity is exactly `2 * v_azimuth` along a **per-frame** azimuth axis (it rotates 12.7 degrees over this collect) — the discriminator between traffic and specular glint. And this otherwise clean public collection carries **two vectors with corrupt `TOA1`/`TOA2`** out of 208,753, concrete support for `CPHDReaderOp`'s per-vector sanity checking. Forming each frame independently also gives a **golden for the cross-tick accumulation path** to diff `ApertureManagerOp`'s signed-weight eviction against.
- **2026-08-06** — [SAR/SIGPROC_PLAN.md](SAR/SIGPROC_PLAN.md) rewritten as the full implementation plan, absorbing the app layout and delivery sequencing that had lived only in chat. **First ship is modes 1–5** plus opt-in autofocus; modes 6–9 stay expressible in the API and are deferred as *packaging*, since none needs an operator or parameter that 1–5 do not already force. The demo is **one executable** `applications/sar_formation/` with a YAML per mode, superseding the earlier `sar_backprojection` naming — modes 1–5 are three `compose()` shapes of one spine, so five executables would be the same graph copied. Phases 0–7 pair each operator slice with the app and data that exercise it; phase 5 is skipped so it is never read as "mode 5".
- Two sequencing consequences worth surfacing outside the SAR docs. The **CPHD subsetter is a phase-2 dependency, not late tooling**: the app streams from a local file and never reaches the network, so the first public collection it reads needs the subsetter first. And the **Capella keys gate more than their own modes** — they carry every `CI4` and every `AmpSF` reader path, so open item 3 in [SAR/INPUT_DATA.md](SAR/INPUT_DATA.md) has to close before phase 1 rather than during it.
- **2026-08-06** — **D24 withdraws the `FMCWWaveformDescriptor` → `LFMWaveformDescriptor` rename.** SAR gets a **new** `LFMWaveformDescriptor` instead and the FMCW type is untouched, so invariant 2's descriptor set goes 6→**9** rather than 6→8. The rename was cheaper on type count and far more expensive on blast radius: the FMCW type is exported from `holoscan.radar`'s `__all__` and named in 57 non-doc files, making it a **breaking change to shipped FMCW public API proposed from inside a SAR contract**, on a modality whose owners are not the ones proposing it. With this, the SAR contract reaches outside SAR in exactly one remaining place — **R10**, the metadata key namespace — which is already flagged for FMCW owners.
- The accepted cost is a duplicated field set that can drift silently: a field added to one waveform type will not fail to compile in the other. Two things keep the intended merge cheap and are conditions of the decision — the new type **mirrors FMCW's field names exactly** rather than adopting SAR vocabulary (`chirp_duration`, not `pulse_duration`), and it already carries the name a merged type should have, so merging later is one sweep (migrate FMCW's call sites, delete the old type) rather than two. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)'s summary of the amendments was stale since D21/D22 landed and has been resynced: four invariants, not three.
- **2026-08-06** — **D22's application-level requirements were wrong and are withdrawn.** Eight documents claimed that composing with this module obliges an app to set `is_metadata_enabled(true)` and a fragment-wide `metadata_policy = kUpdate`. Neither holds in the installed SDK headers: metadata is **enabled by default** (`kDefaultMetadataEnabled = true`), and `metadata_policy` is **per-operator, taking precedence** over the fragment default. `kUpdate` is still mechanically forced, but for the one consumer merging several ports from a single producer — `BackprojectionOp` — which sets it on itself. Our own [SDK_QUESTIONS.md](SDK_QUESTIONS.md) had it right all along ("`MetadataPolicy` is per operator"); the SAR docs contradicted it.
- The correction matters beyond tidiness because it repriced an amendment under review: the metadata plane in **R1** was carrying a stated cost of two app-wide settings, and it actually costs the application nothing. What survives is a hazard rather than a requirement — a foreign operator with metadata *explicitly* disabled between the source and the control owner drops the boundary announcement, surfacing as `BackprojectionOp`'s `stop()` warning. [SAR/TESTING.md](SAR/TESTING.md)'s assertion is retargeted to match: not "the app set these", but "the module configures itself and degrades visibly when something external interferes."
- **2026-08-06** — **`BackprojectionOp.precision_mode` is renamed `backend`**, a contract change to a parameter name, closing **I6**'s naming half; the value set stays open until kernels exist and the frontier is measured. The old name claimed too little: the parameter selects a strategy of which precision is one consequence among throughput, memory and admissibility, some variants being valid only for planar scenes. The contract had already called the name provisional. Renaming is free now — no SAR code exists — and would not be later. Known residual: in a GPU library `backend` can read as a device selector, which it is not, so the parameter note says so. The old name stands in the two practitioner review records; I6 carries the mapping.

- **2026-08-07** — **R1 closes: the SAR contract is accepted and implementation begins.** The team's call was to treat the design documents on this branch as approved and build against them, which lifts the gate in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) and [SAR/SIGPROC_PLAN.md](SAR/SIGPROC_PLAN.md) that had held back SAR operator code. Acceptance ratifies the five invariant amendments, the two new conventions, and D1–D24 along with them; the invariants are now recorded in `AGENTS.md` as amended for SAR rather than as proposals under review.
- Acceptance came with two conditions, and they are the live constraint now. The contract may be **revised freely** as implementation teaches us things — that was explicit, not grudging — but **no revision is silent**: each is tagged at the point of change, appended to a running log in [SAR/OPERATOR_API.md](SAR/OPERATOR_API.md) → *Contract changes since approval*, and stated to the developer in the reply that makes it. A revision reaching **beyond SAR** clears a higher bar: explicit agreement before it lands, plus a row in the module-wide [TEAM_BLOCKED.md](TEAM_BLOCKED.md) so FMCW owners see it without reading SAR docs. Where a SAR-scoped and a beyond-SAR option both solve a problem, the SAR-scoped one wins by default — D24 is the worked example.
- **R10 is the one open item that survived**, and contract acceptance sharpened rather than settled it. Its two options stopped being symmetric: module-level placement of the metadata key namespace is now a beyond-SAR change needing agreement, while the `sar` tier is SAR-scoped and needs nobody's. Since `D22`'s `stream_boundary` key is reached in phases 1 and 3, the standing instruction is to use `holoscan.radar.sar.stream_boundary` behind one named constant until the team rules — committing FMCW to nothing and leaving promotion a one-line change.

Parameterizing the order is deferred (ClusterDBSCAN OPEN_ITEMS §8) | 2026-07-22 |
| L10 — ClusterDBSCAN cluster-id numbering order | Pin **descending point count** (cluster 0 = largest), ties broken by **ascending component root** (lowest member row index); `sizes` non-increasing. Chosen over the prior ascending-root numbering — descending size gives a "cluster 0 is largest" guarantee and makes the `max_clusters` cap keep the largest (L7) for free. Deterministic (`labels` a pure function of `(points, epsilon, min_points)`). Implemented via a host argsort of per-id counts + a `remap_labels` GPU pass; `OPERATOR_API.md` `labels`/`sizes` updated; tested (`NumbersClustersByDescendingSize`). **Deferred:** a `cluster_order` param for alternate orders (OPEN_ITEMS §8) | 2026-07-22 |
| L6 — CFAR exact-sized `detection_idx` | **Ratify exact sizing** (chosen over a capacity + `num_detections` port). Accept the per-tick scalar device→host count sync as the cost of the contract: it moves one integer, and the exact-size, no-companion shape is load-bearing across the downstream chain (`SnapshotGather`/`FeatureAssembly`/`DetectionReduction`/`ClusterDBSCAN` all positionally join on "row count *is* `num_detections`"), so breaking it is a broad change unjustified without a measured latency budget the sync violates. ## Roadmap (deferred capabilities)

Capabilities deliberately **out of the current scope** — settled as deferred (no
current consumer / larger project), not blocked. Each is enforced today with a
clear error or documented limitation; pick one up when a concrete consumer
needs it.

| Capability | Why deferred | Where tracked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Staggered / non-uniform TDM schedules** (varying TX dwell or count) | Needs a per-TX-timing schedule descriptor **and** a generalized `MIMOCoherentCompensationOp` model (the compensation for arbitrary timing is an open design question). Uniform round-robin covers the FMCW workflows; non-uniform is rejected with a clear error today. Settled L12 | [MIMODeinterleave OPEN_ITEMS B.1](../../operators/fmcw/mimo_deinterleave_op/OPEN_ITEMS.md) |
| **Sum-set ("Harnessing Holes") holey subarray smoothing** | S3 selects a contiguous filled ULA/URA today; a sum-set decomposition (`S_b + S_c`, keeping holes for aperture when the array is sum-set smoothable) would recover more DOF / aperture from holey MIMO co-arrays. Research item — no consumer, and the gridless estimators (Root-MUSIC/ESPRIT) aren't built yet. Settled L14 (fork 3) | [SubarraySelection OPEN_ITEMS](../../operators/angle_estimation/subarray_selection_op/OPEN_ITEMS.md) |
| **`CoordinateFrame` for the FMCW operators** | The descriptor is SAR-only today, and deliberately so: invariant 6 keeps FMCW entirely in the array-local frame, so there is no second frame for a declaration to disambiguate and adding one would be ceremony. It becomes real the moment platform motion or geolocation enters the FMCW side — a moving-platform automotive or airborne configuration, or any product that has to be placed on a map. Revisit once SAR lands and the descriptor has been exercised against real collections | [SAR/OPERATOR_API.md](SAR/OPERATOR_API.md) → Supporting data structures → CoordinateFrame |
| **`sinc` / `nearest` backprojection interpolation** | `BackprojectionOp` implements `linear` only. The reference implementation spans ~48 tuned kernel variants and every one interpolates with two taps, because the interpolation is phase-controlled (envelope interpolated, carrier phase applied from the exact range) and `SARRangeCompressionOp.nfft` oversampling already buys impulse-response quality upstream. Both values are named in the enum and rejected with a clear error today, so implementing either later is a behavior change on a previously-erroring value rather than a contract break | [SAR/OPERATOR_API.md](SAR/OPERATOR_API.md) → BackprojectionOp → Notes |
